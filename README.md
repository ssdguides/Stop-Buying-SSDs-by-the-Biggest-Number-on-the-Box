You open two tabs. One drive promises 14,800 MB/s. The other promises 7,300 MB/s and costs a lot less. Your laptop or motherboard may never let either drive show what it can do.

The honest summary of our research is that moving from a hard drive to any SSD is the biggest jump most people will feel. Moving from a good Gen4 drive to a Gen5 drive is usually much smaller.

So start with your device and your work, then pick.

If you want one dependable drive, the Samsung 990 PRO sits near the practical ceiling of PCIe 4.0, with strong endurance ratings and compatibility with older slots.

If you move huge files all day on a Gen5 board, the Samsung 9100 PRO is faster on paper, but runs warmer and only pays off with a true Gen5 slot and good cooling.

If you game, the WD_BLACK SN850X is a proven Gen4 choice with a heatsink option for the PS5. Loading times depend heavily on your CPU, memory and the game itself, so a faster drive doesn’t mean proportionally faster load screens.

If you’re on a budget, the WD Blue SN580 is cheap, cool and efficient, though it’s DRAM-less and slows on long writes.

If you’re upgrading a laptop, the Samsung 990 EVO Plus balances speed and power efficiency. Check that your slot supports NVMe, takes a 2280 drive, and has clearance.

If you want external storage, the SanDisk Extreme PRO USB4 is fast only on a USB4 or Thunderbolt port.

If your computer is older, a 2.5-inch SATA drive like the Samsung 870 EVO is still a worthwhile upgrade.

Three more cautions. M.2 is a shape, not a speed, so check whether your slot takes NVMe, SATA or both. A Gen5 drive in a Gen4 slot runs at Gen4 speed. And 2026 prices are volatile, so compare price per terabyte and check live prices.
